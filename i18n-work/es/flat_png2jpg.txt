41	Progressive versus baseline JPG, and other technical details
42	The JPGs created here are standard baseline JPEG files with 4:2:0 chroma subsampling at normal quality settings, which is what cameras, phones and nearly every website produce. They open in every image viewer, browser, word processor, print kiosk and upload form, including very old software. Colour is stored in the sRGB colour space. PNG files with 16 bits per channel are reduced to 8 bits, which is all JPG supports and is more than enough for viewing and printing. Images with an embedded gamma or colour profile are converted to sRGB by the browser before encoding, so colours appear the same as they do when you view the PNG in your browser.

68	What happens to transparency?
69	PNG supports an alpha channel, so a logo can have a transparent background that shows whatever is behind it. JPG has no alpha channel. When converting, every partly or fully transparent pixel is blended onto the background colour you choose. Semi-transparent edges – the soft anti-aliased outline of a logo – are blended smoothly, so there is no jagged halo.
70	Choose the background to match where the JPG will be used: white for documents and most websites, black for dark-mode designs, or your page's exact colour for a seamless look. If you actually need transparency in a smaller file, convert to WebP instead, which supports transparency and lossy compression.
71	Why PNG files are so big
72	PNG compresses images by finding repeated patterns of pixels, which works wonderfully for flat areas of colour – a logo on white, a screenshot of a spreadsheet – but poorly for photos, where nearly every pixel is slightly different from its neighbour. A 12-megapixel photo has 36 million colour values; PNG has to store essentially all of them. JPG instead describes small blocks of the image as smooth patterns and throws away detail too fine to see, which is why it is so much smaller for photos.
73	Screenshots on modern high-resolution screens are also very large – a Retina MacBook or 4K monitor screenshot can be 3000–4000 pixels wide. Setting a maximum width of 1600 or 1920 while converting reduces size dramatically and still looks sharp on normal screens.
