47	Composition tips
48	Follow the rule of thirds: put eyes or the horizon on an imaginary line one third in from the edge.
49	Leave a little space in the direction a person is looking or moving.
50	Crop out distracting edges – bins, cables, half-visible people – rather than the subject.
51	Avoid cutting people at the joints (wrists, knees, ankles).
52	Cropping removes pixels: start from the largest original so the crop stays sharp when printed.
53	Crop vs resize vs trim
54	Cropping removes part of the picture and changes its shape; resizing keeps the whole picture and changes its pixel dimensions; *trimming* usually means removing plain borders automatically. To make a round profile picture use [Circle Crop Image](/circle-crop-image/); to split one image into pieces use [Image Splitter](/image-splitter/).

85	Common problems and fixes
86	**The file will not open** – check it is a real image and not a shortcut or a cloud placeholder; download it fully from iCloud, Google Photos or OneDrive first.
87	**The result looks blurry** – start from the original, not a thumbnail sent through WhatsApp or Messenger, which recompress photos heavily.
88	**Colours look slightly different** – wide-gamut (Display P3) photos are shown in sRGB, the standard for the web; this is normal and consistent across devices.
89	**The download does not appear on iPhone** – look in Files → Downloads, or long-press the preview and choose Save to Photos.
90	**A big batch is slow** – very large photos use a lot of memory; close other tabs or process 20–30 images at a time.
91	Privacy
92	Everything happens inside your browser tab. Your images are read from your disk into memory, processed there and saved straight back to your device. Nothing is uploaded, stored or logged, there is no account, and the tool keeps working offline once the page has loaded – so it is safe for private, confidential and unreleased material.
