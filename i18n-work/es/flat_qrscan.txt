71	Stay safe: QR phishing ("quishing")
72	Fake QR codes stuck over parking meters, sent in e-mails or printed on flyers are a growing scam: they lead to look-alike payment or login pages. This scanner never opens a link automatically. Before you click, read the address: check the spelling of the domain, be wary of URL shorteners and unusual endings, and never enter bank or login details on a page reached from an unexpected code. Payment requests should be paid inside your banking or payment app, not on a web page opened from a sticker.
73	Why won't my QR code scan?
74	The image is too blurry or the code is too small – crop closer or take the photo again in good light.
75	Part of the code is cut off or covered; all three corner squares must be visible.
76	Very low contrast or coloured backgrounds – the scanner tries inverted colours, but a pale code on a busy photo may still fail.
77	Heavy logos or damaged print exceed the code's error correction.
78	It is not a QR code: Data Matrix, Aztec and PDF417 are read only where the browser's barcode API supports them.

103	How a QR code stores data
104	A QR (Quick Response) code is a two-dimensional matrix code invented by Denso Wave in 1994 for tracking car parts. The three large squares in the corners tell the scanner where the code is and which way up it is; the smaller alignment square and the timing patterns help it correct for perspective and curved surfaces. The remaining modules hold the data plus Reed–Solomon error-correction codes, which is why a code can still be read when part of it is dirty, damaged or covered by a logo – up to about 7 %, 15 %, 25 % or 30 % depending on the error-correction level chosen when it was made. A single QR code can store up to 4,296 alphanumeric characters or 7,089 digits, although most codes hold a short web link.
105	Reading Wi-Fi, contact and event codes
106	Many QR codes contain structured text rather than a link. A Wi-Fi code looks like WIFI:T:WPA;S:NetworkName;P:password;; – the network name follows S: and the password follows P:, so you can copy the password to a laptop that cannot scan codes itself. Contact codes contain a vCard (BEGIN:VCARD … END:VCARD) or MeCard; copy the text into a .vcf file to import it into your address book. Event codes contain a VEVENT with the title, start and end times that you can paste into a calendar. Payment codes start with a scheme such as bitcoin: or upi: followed by the address and amount – always confirm the recipient inside your wallet or banking app.

137	Tips for a clean scan
138	Fill the frame: the code should cover at least a quarter of the image width.
139	Avoid glare on glossy paper and screens – tilt slightly or turn off a nearby lamp.
140	Keep the camera steady for a second; the scanner checks four frames per second.
141	For codes printed on curved bottles or cans, photograph straight on.
142	Clean the camera lens – a smudge is the most common cause of failed scans on phones.
