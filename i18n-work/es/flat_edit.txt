23	Most free online PDF editors are really annotation tools: they let you draw boxes on top of the page but not change the words that are already there. Others do edit text but send your document to a server and limit you to a few tasks an hour – Sejda, for example, allows 3 tasks per hour and 50 MB per file on its free tier. {brand} is different on both counts. You can change existing text directly, and the whole editor runs inside your browser tab, so the document stays on your device and there is no hourly limit.
24	The editor opens your PDF with PDF.js, finds every text run along with its font, size and colour, and lets you click on any of them to type. When you save, the original text is removed and your new text is written back in a matching font, at the same position. Images, vector graphics and the rest of the page are left untouched, so the file looks just like it did – only with your changes.

53	Editing scanned PDFs with OCR
54	A scanned page is a picture, so there is no text to click on. Run OCR from the editor first: it recognises the words on the page in your chosen language and turns them into editable text boxes positioned over the original. You can then correct, replace or white-out text as with any other PDF. For a searchable but otherwise unchanged copy of a scan, use [OCR PDF](/ocr-pdf/).

63	Fonts and how close the match is
64	When you edit existing text, the editor tries to use the same font that is embedded in the PDF. If the PDF only contains a subset of a font (only the letters that were used), new letters may not exist in it; in that case the editor falls back to the closest standard font – serif, sans-serif or monospace – in the same weight and style. For most business documents in Arial, Helvetica, Times or Calibri the result is indistinguishable. You can always choose a different font from the toolbar.
