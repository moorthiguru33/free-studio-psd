56	Fill or fit?
57	*Fill* crops each photo to the same cell shape, giving a tidy, magazine-like grid – faces near the edges may be cut. *Fit* shows every photo whole and adds background around it – better for documents, screenshots and artwork. A white or soft-grey background with 10–20 px spacing looks clean; black works well for night photos and dramatic images.

62	Web pages: 1200–1600 px wide, then [Compress Image](/compress-image/).

70	Collage vs grid split

72	Printing your collage
73	For a printed poster or photo book page, set the width from the print size: inches × 300 for sharp prints (an 8 × 10 in print needs 2400 px across), or centimetres × 118. Choose PNG or high-quality JPG, leave at least 20 px spacing so the cut lines between photos stay even, and keep important faces away from the outer edge in case the printer trims a few millimetres. Most photo labs accept JPG files up to 25–50 MB.

80	Colour and layout harmony
81	A collage looks deliberate when the photos share something: a dominant colour, similar brightness, or the same subject at different moments. Alternate close-ups with wide shots so the eye has places to rest, and avoid placing two very busy pictures next to each other. If one photo is much darker, brighten it with [Photo Filters](/photo-filters/) before combining. Even spacing and a neutral background make mixed photos feel like a set.
82	Collages for business
83	Online shops use collages to show every colour of a product in one image; estate agents combine rooms into one listing picture; event organisers share highlights the next morning; teachers make visual timetables and classroom displays. 84	Choosing the right photos
85	Start with more pictures than you need and cut ruthlessly: a collage of six strong images beats one of twenty average ones. Mix orientations carefully – in Fill mode portrait photos lose their top and bottom, and landscape photos lose their sides, so check that heads and key details stay inside each cell. Group photos look best in larger cells, so for a feature image use fewer columns. When telling a story, such as a trip or a renovation, order the photos in time so viewers read them naturally from the first cell to the last. If two photos are nearly identical, keep only the sharper one; repetition weakens a grid more than an empty cell would, and fewer, larger photos are easier to read on a small phone screen.
86	Spacing and background explained
87	Spacing is the gap in pixels between photos and around the outer edge. 0 px gives a seamless mosaic where photos touch; 5–10 px looks modern and tidy on phones; 20–40 px gives a gallery or scrapbook feel and leaves room for printing. The background colour fills the gaps and – in Fit mode – the space around photos that do not match the cell shape. Pick a colour taken from the photos themselves with the [Image Color Picker](/image-color-picker/) for a coordinated, designed look.
88	Quality tips
89	Use photos with similar lighting and colour for a calm result, or apply the same look with [Photo Filters](/photo-filters/) first. Put the strongest image in the top-left or centre cell, where eyes land first. HEIC photos from iPhones are converted automatically.

120	Common problems and fixes
121	**The file will not open** – check it is a real image and not a shortcut or a cloud placeholder; download it fully from iCloud, Google Photos or OneDrive first.
122	**The result looks blurry** – start from the original, not a thumbnail sent through WhatsApp or Messenger, which recompress photos heavily.
123	**Colours look slightly different** – wide-gamut (Display P3) photos are shown in sRGB, the standard for the web; this is normal and consistent across devices.
124	**The download does not appear on iPhone** – look in Files → Downloads, or long-press the preview and choose Save to Photos.
125	**A big batch is slow** – very large photos use a lot of memory; close other tabs or process 20–30 images at a time.
