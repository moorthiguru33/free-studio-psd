48	Static vs dynamic QR codes
49	There are two kinds of QR codes on the market. A **static** code encodes your content directly – the URL, the Wi-Fi details – in the black and white pattern. A **dynamic** code encodes a short link to the provider's server, which then redirects to your real URL. Dynamic codes let you change the destination later and count scans, but they only work while you keep paying the provider: if the subscription lapses, or the provider closes, every printed code stops working. Many 'free' generators create dynamic codes by default and deactivate them after a trial period – a frequent source of frustration for restaurants and small businesses whose menus suddenly stopped scanning.

115	Designing a QR code that always scans
116	**Keep strong contrast.** Dark modules on a light background. Light-on-dark (inverted) codes are not read by some older scanners.
117	**Don't over-style.** Dots and gradients look great; check that the darkest and lightest colours still differ clearly.
118	**Keep the quiet zone.** The white margin around the code (at least four modules) helps cameras find it – don't crop it away.
119	**Logos up to about 20–25% of the area.** Larger logos hide too many modules even at level H.
120	**Shorter content = bigger modules.** A short URL produces a simpler code that scans from further away.
121	**Always test** with two or three phones – iPhone camera, Android camera and a scanner app – before printing thousands of copies.
122	Error correction levels
123	QR codes contain redundant data so they still work when part of the code is damaged, dirty or covered by a logo. There are four levels: L recovers about 7% of damaged data, M about 15%, Q about 25% and H about 30%. Higher levels make the code denser. Use M for clean digital use, Q for outdoor print, and H when you add a logo – {brand} switches to H automatically when you upload one.
124	Print size and scanning distance
125	A useful rule of thumb is that a QR code can be scanned from about ten times its width: a 2 cm code from 20 cm, a 10 cm code on a poster from about 1 metre, and a 1 m code on a banner from about 10 metres. For business cards, 2–2.5 cm is the practical minimum. For printing, use SVG or PDF so the edges stay perfectly sharp at any size, or PNG at 2000 pixels or more.

140	Wi-Fi QR codes
141	A Wi-Fi code lets guests join your network by pointing their camera at it – no spelling out long passwords. Enter the network name (SSID) exactly as it appears, the password, and the security type (WPA/WPA2/WPA3 for almost all modern routers, WEP for very old ones, or none for open networks). Tick 'hidden' if your network does not broadcast its name. iPhones (iOS 11 and later) and Android phones (10 and later) support Wi-Fi codes from the built-in camera. 147	How a QR code works
148	QR stands for 'Quick Response'. The format was invented in 1994 by Denso Wave, a Toyota group company, to track car parts, and is now an international standard (ISO/IEC 18004). A QR code is a grid of square modules. The three large squares in the corners – the finder patterns or 'eyes' – let the camera locate the code and work out its angle. Smaller alignment patterns correct for perspective, timing lines set the grid, and format information tells the reader which error-correction level and mask are used. The rest of the grid holds your data, encoded with Reed–Solomon error correction, the same mathematics that lets scratched CDs play.
149	Codes come in 40 versions, from 21×21 modules (version 1) to 177×177 (version 40). The more data you encode and the higher the error correction, the larger the version – which is why a long URL with a logo produces a denser code than a short link without one. A version 40 code can hold up to about 4,296 alphanumeric characters, but for reliable scanning on phones, staying well below a few hundred characters is wise.

176	PNG, SVG or PDF?
177	**PNG** is a pixel image, perfect for websites, slides, social posts and e-mail. Choose a size large enough for the use – 1000 pixels for screens, 2000–4000 for print. **SVG** is a vector format: the code is stored as shapes, so it can be enlarged to any size, from a sticker to a building wrap, without blur. Designers can open it in Illustrator, Inkscape, Figma or Affinity and change colours. **PDF** is ready to send to a printer or to place in a document; when you use gradients, frames or a logo, the PDF contains a high-resolution image of your styled code.
178	QR codes for restaurants and cafés

180	Packaging, products and labels
181	On product packaging, QR codes link to instructions, ingredient lists, warranty registration, videos or reorder pages. Use a short URL so the code stays small and simple, keep it away from folds and curved edges, and use SVG so the printer gets perfectly sharp edges. 182	Events, flyers and posters
183	Event codes can add the date, time and place directly to a phone's calendar. Location codes open the venue in the map app. On posters, put the code at chest height, add a clear call to action ('Scan for tickets') in the frame, and make sure there is enough light where the poster hangs. Codes behind glass work, but avoid reflective lamination that creates glare.
184	Safety: QR code scams
185	Because people cannot read a QR code by eye, criminals sometimes stick fake codes over real ones on parking meters, posters or restaurant tables to send people to phishing pages – a scam sometimes called 'quishing'. If you place codes in public, check them regularly, consider tamper-evident stickers, and print your domain name under the code so people can see where it leads. When you scan a code yourself, check the URL preview your phone shows before opening it, and never enter passwords or payment details on a page reached from an unexpected code. You can check any code's contents safely with our [QR Code Scanner](/qr-code-scanner/).

202	How to scan a QR code
203	On an **iPhone**, open the Camera app and point it at the code; a banner with the link appears – tap it. You can also add the Code Scanner to Control Centre. On **Android**, open the camera (most phones since Android 9 scan automatically) or use Google Lens from the search bar or camera app. On a **computer**, upload a screenshot or photo of the code to our [QR Code Scanner](/qr-code-scanner/). Wi-Fi, contact and event codes trigger the matching action – join network, save contact, add to calendar – instead of opening a link.
204	Using a logo well
205	A logo makes a code instantly recognisable and increases trust, but it covers part of the data. Use a simple, high-contrast version of your logo – an icon or monogram rather than a full wordmark – on a square or circular image, ideally with a white or transparent background. Keep it to roughly a fifth of the code's width. Since the code relies on error correction to work around the logo, avoid combining a large logo with very long content, which makes modules small and leaves less margin for error. If you only have a logo with a busy background, cut it out first with [Remove Background](/remove-background/).
206	Frames and calls to action
207	A bare QR code tells people nothing about what they will get; a short instruction next to it gives them a reason to pick up their phone. Use the frame option to add a label such as 'Scan for menu', 'Scan to join Wi-Fi', 'Scan for 10% off' or 'Watch the video'. The 'label below' frame adds a text strip under the code; 'box with label' surrounds the code with a coloured border, which also guarantees a clean quiet zone when the code is placed on busy backgrounds or photographs. Keep the label to two to four words so it stays readable at small sizes.
208	Accessibility
209	Not everyone can scan a code – some people have older phones, visual impairments or simply prefer not to. Whenever space allows, print a short, readable URL or phone number next to the code as an alternative, and use descriptive alt text when you place a QR image on a website. On screens, a QR code is mainly useful for moving from a desktop to a phone; on the phone itself, a normal link is always easier than a code.
210	QR codes in documents and presentations
211	Adding a QR code to a slide lets the audience open your resources, survey or contact page while you are still speaking. Place it in a corner of the final slide, at least a fifth of the slide height, and leave it on screen long enough for people at the back to scan. In reports, CVs and printed handouts, a small code beside a link saves readers from typing long addresses. Download PNG at 1000 pixels for PowerPoint, Google Slides or Keynote, or SVG for Word and InDesign layouts where it must stay crisp. You can also put a code into an existing PDF – such as a flyer or ticket – with [Add Image to PDF](/add-image-to-pdf/). For multi-page documents, consider a different code on each section page so readers jump straight to the relevant online material instead of a general homepage.
