How to Change a Studio Background in Photoshop

Replacing a plain or messy background with a clean studio backdrop is one of the most common jobs in a photo studio, from passport photos to kids portraits and festival prints. With modern Photoshop it takes a few minutes. Here is a reliable method.

By Guru Moorthi · Updated 2026-10-19 · 3 min read

1. Open the photo and the backdrop

Open your portrait and a studio background PSD. Check that the background is at least as large as your final print; a 12x18 print at 300 DPI needs 5400 x 3600 pixels.

2. Select the subject

In the portrait, choose Select > Subject. Photoshop's AI selection handles most people well. Then open Select > Select and Mask. Use the Refine Hair button or the Refine Edge Brush over hair and fabric edges. Set Output To: New Layer with Layer Mask and click OK.

3. Move the subject onto the backdrop

Drag the masked layer into the background document. Press Ctrl+T to scale and position the subject. Keep feet or the bottom edge consistent with the floor line of the backdrop so the person does not look like they are floating.

4. Match light and colour

This is what separates a believable result from an obvious cut-out:

  • Add a Curves adjustment layer clipped to the subject (Alt+Ctrl+G) and match brightness and contrast to the backdrop.
  • Use a clipped Color Balance or Photo Filter layer to match warmth. A warm golden backdrop with a cool blue subject never looks right.
  • Check the light direction. If the backdrop has light from the left, the subject's highlights should be on the left too; flip the backdrop horizontally if needed.

5. Add a soft shadow

Create a new layer below the subject, paint a soft black shadow under the feet or behind the shoulders with a large soft brush at low opacity, and lower the layer opacity until it looks natural. Contact shadows are what make the subject 'sit' in the scene.

6. Clean up edges

Zoom to 100% and look for halos of the old background around hair and shoulders. Paint on the layer mask with a soft black brush to remove them, or use Layer > Matting > Defringe on a duplicated, merged copy.

7. Export

Flatten a copy and export as JPG in sRGB for printing or sharing. Keep the layered file in case the client wants a different backdrop; with the subject masked you can swap backgrounds in seconds.

Tips for faster studio work

  • Shoot against a plain, evenly lit wall to make selections cleaner.
  • Keep a folder of favourite backdrops sorted by colour and mood.
  • Record an action for the Curves and Color Balance layers you use most often.

Lighting the shoot for an easy swap

A background change is much easier when it is planned at the shoot. Use a plain, evenly lit backdrop in a colour that does not appear in the clothes, keep the subject a few feet in front of it to avoid shadows and colour spill, and light the backdrop separately if you can. A small rim light on hair and shoulders gives Select Subject a clean edge to find. Our studio background setup tips go into lighting and backdrop choice.

Choosing the new background

Pick a replacement whose light direction and perspective match the photo: a background lit from the left will look wrong behind a subject lit from the right. A subtle Gaussian Blur on the background layer helps it sit behind the subject like a real backdrop.
