How Many Pages Should a Wedding Album Have?

Clients often ask how many pages a wedding album should have, and the honest answer is: enough to tell the story of the events they care about, and no more than their budget and the photos allow. Here is a practical way to decide, along with the terms that confuse many clients.

By Guru Moorthi · Updated 2026-10-20 · 3 min read

Sheets, spreads and pages

In panoramic albums such as 12x36, one printed sheet usually forms one open spread (two facing pages). Labs often price albums by the number of sheets, while clients think in pages. Thirty sheets means about sixty page sides. Always state which unit you are quoting, both in your price list and on the order form, to avoid misunderstandings.

Start from the events

List every event the album must cover and give each a number of sheets based on its importance. For example, an album might allocate a few sheets to preparations, several to the main ceremony and rituals, one or two to couple portraits, one or two to family groups and several to the reception. Add an opening sheet with the couple's best portrait and names, and a closing sheet. The total gives you a realistic page count.

Then check the photos

Count the strong photos for each event. If an event has only a handful of good images, giving it many sheets will force you to use weak photos or repeat similar shots. If an event has many strong images, consider adding a sheet or moving extra photos to an online gallery. Our guide on how many photos to put in an album explains how to balance this.

Budget and binding limits

More sheets increase the cost and the thickness of the book. Very thick albums are heavy and can strain the binding. Labs often have a minimum and maximum sheet count for each binding type and paper thickness; ask before quoting.

Typical choices by album type

  • Main wedding album: many studios offer packages with a fixed number of sheets and allow clients to add more.
  • Parent albums: fewer sheets, focusing on family moments and key rituals.
  • Pre-wedding albums: usually shorter, with larger photos and fewer per spread.
  • Reception or engagement albums: short albums dedicated to one event.

These are common practices rather than fixed rules; your package structure should reflect your style and your clients.

Offer upgrades, not cramming

When a couple wants to include more photos than the package allows, offer extra sheets at a clear price per sheet, or a second smaller album. This keeps the design clean and creates a fair upsell, instead of squeezing ten photos onto every spread.

Plan with a template family

A simple worked example

Suppose a couple wants coverage of the engagement, the wedding day and the reception, and the lab's package is 30 sheets. You might give one sheet to the opening portrait, four to the engagement, three to preparations, eight to the muhurtham and rituals, two to couple portraits, three to family groups, seven to the reception, one to details and one to a closing page. If the reception has fewer strong photos than planned, move a sheet to family groups. Numbers like these are only a starting point for the conversation with the couple.

Once the sheet count is agreed, choose one template family per event so the album feels consistent.
