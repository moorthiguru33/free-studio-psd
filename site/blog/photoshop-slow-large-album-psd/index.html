Fix Photoshop Running Slow with Album PSDs

A finished 12x36 album sheet with a dozen high-resolution photos, textures and adjustment layers can easily pass 1 GB. When Photoshop starts lagging, freezing or showing scratch disk errors, a few settings and habits make a big difference. This guide covers practical fixes, starting with the free ones.

By Guru Moorthi · Updated 2026-10-23 · 4 min read

Check memory and scratch disk settings

RAM allocation

Open Edit > Preferences > Performance (Photoshop > Settings on Mac). Let Photoshop use 70 to 80 percent of available RAM. With 8 GB of RAM, large album files will struggle; 16 GB is a practical minimum for 12x36 work, and 32 GB is comfortable.

Scratch disk

Photoshop uses a scratch disk when RAM runs out. In Preferences > Scratch Disks, choose your fastest drive with plenty of free space, ideally an SSD with 50 GB or more free. A full C drive is one of the most common reasons for the scratch disks are full error.

Reduce history and cache

In Performance preferences, lower History States from 50 to around 20. Each state stores data, and big files fill memory quickly. Cache Levels of 4 to 6 suit large documents. Use Edit > Purge > All occasionally during long sessions to free memory (note that it clears undo history).

Make photos the right size before placing

This is the biggest win. A 45-megapixel camera file placed into a small frame still carries all its pixels. Resize photos to the size they will be printed: a photo covering a third of a 12x36 sheet needs about 3600 pixels on its long side. Batch resize with File > Scripts > Image Processor, or use an action described in Photoshop actions for album designers.

Use Smart Objects wisely

Smart Objects protect quality but add file size. Embedded Smart Objects holding full camera files are heavy; linked Smart Objects keep the PSD smaller. Once a photo is final, you can rasterise it on a working copy to lighten the file. Read Smart Objects for album design for the trade-offs.

Simplify the layer stack

  • Merge finished adjustments: if colour corrections are final, merge them into the photo on a copy.
  • Delete hidden layers: unused alternatives and old versions still take memory.
  • Avoid many layer effects: dozens of drop shadows and glows slow redraw.
  • Work in 8-bit: 16-bit doubles memory use and is rarely needed for album sheets.

Graphics processor settings

Enable Use Graphics Processor in Performance preferences if your GPU is supported. Update graphics drivers. If you see display glitches, try the Advanced Settings and choose Basic mode.

Save smarter

Saving huge files takes time. In File Handling preferences, turn off Maximize PSD and PSB File Compatibility if you do not need it, which makes saving faster and files smaller. Files above 2 GB must be saved as PSB (Large Document Format).

Work from a local drive

Editing directly from a USB drive, network folder or cloud sync folder slows everything and risks corrupting files. Copy the template and photos to a local SSD, work there, and back up afterwards.

When to upgrade hardware

If you have applied these fixes and Photoshop still struggles, the order of upgrades that help most is: more RAM, an SSD for the system and scratch disk, then a better GPU.

A quick diagnostic checklist

When Photoshop slows down, work through this list before changing hardware:

  • Check Efficiency: in the status bar at the bottom of the document, choose Efficiency. Below 100 percent means Photoshop is using the scratch disk heavily.
  • Check document size: the status bar also shows document size. If it is several gigabytes, resize photos or remove hidden layers.
  • Close other documents: every open PSD uses memory.
  • Close background apps: browsers with many tabs and video editors compete for RAM.
  • Restart Photoshop: long sessions accumulate memory use; a restart often restores speed.

Reset preferences if things break

If Photoshop becomes unstable even with small files, preferences may be corrupted. Hold Alt+Ctrl+Shift (Option+Cmd+Shift on Mac) immediately after launching Photoshop and confirm deleting the settings file. Note your custom shortcuts and workspaces first, because they will reset.

Keep Photoshop updated, carefully

Updates often include performance fixes, but avoid updating in the middle of wedding season deadlines. Wait a week or two after a major release, check that your plugins still work, and keep the previous version installed if your Creative Cloud plan allows.

Autosave and recovery

Large files take time to autosave, which can cause brief freezes. In File Handling preferences you can set the auto-recovery interval to 10 or 15 minutes instead of 5. Do not switch it off completely; losing an hour of work on a complex spread is worse than a short pause.

Frequently asked questions

How much RAM do I need for 12x36 album design?

16 GB is a practical minimum; 32 GB is comfortable for large sheets with many photos.

How do I fix the scratch disks are full error?

Free space on the scratch drive or select a different drive with 50 GB or more free in Preferences > Scratch Disks.

What is a PSB file?

PSB is Photoshop's Large Document Format, used for files over 2 GB or larger than 30,000 pixels in either dimension.

Why does Photoshop freeze when saving large album files?

Large files take time to compress and write. Turning off Maximize Compatibility and saving to an SSD helps.

Does closing other programs help Photoshop speed?

Yes. Browsers and other heavy apps use RAM that Photoshop could otherwise use for large documents.
