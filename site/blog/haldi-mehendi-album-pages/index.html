Haldi and Mehendi Album Page Design Ideas

Haldi and mehendi functions produce some of the most colourful and candid photos of a wedding. They deserve album pages with a different energy from the formal ceremony. This guide covers how to design haldi and mehendi spreads that feel joyful while still matching the rest of the album.

By Guru Moorthi · Updated 2026-10-24 · 4 min read

Why these pages need a different approach

Ceremony spreads are usually symmetrical, formal and calm. Haldi and mehendi photos are full of movement, laughter, bright yellow and green, and close-up details. Using the same layout for both makes one or the other look wrong. Plan these sections as a distinct chapter with its own rhythm, but keep fonts and margins consistent with the album.

Haldi spreads

Colour palette

Turmeric yellow, marigold orange and fresh green dominate. Use backgrounds in cream, pale yellow or soft white so the photos provide the colour. A heavy yellow background with yellow-stained faces becomes overwhelming.

Layout ideas

  • Action sequence: three or four frames in a row showing haldi being applied, the reaction and the laughter.
  • Hero candid: one large photo of the bride or groom covered in haldi, with small detail shots of the bowl, flowers and hands.
  • Family fun collage: a tighter grid of relatives joining in.

Mehendi spreads

Showing the design

The mehendi pattern is the star. Include at least one sharp close-up of the hands and feet, ideally in a large frame. Clients often want to see the full design, including the hidden name of the partner.

Mood and colours

Greens, deep oranges and jewel tones suit mehendi evenings. Floral templates work beautifully; browse the floral album PSD tag.

Dance and music

Mehendi nights often include dancing. Use dynamic layouts with tilted frames or overlapping photos, but sparingly, so the page does not look cluttered.

Matching photos with different lighting

Haldi is usually shot in daylight, mehendi often under mixed evening lighting. Correct white balance first so skin looks natural, then apply a consistent grade. See our colour correction tips and colour grading guide. Yellow haldi on skin can confuse auto white balance; set it manually from a neutral white cloth or wall.

How many pages to give

For most albums, two to four spreads for haldi and two to four for mehendi is enough. More than that and the main ceremony feels rushed. Our guide on how many pages a wedding album needs explains how to balance sections.

Captions and titles

A small chapter title like Haldi or Mehendi Night at the start of each section helps viewers. Keep it short and use the album's main script font. For ideas, read wedding album captions and quotes.

Templates to use

Sangeet and cocktail nights

Many weddings combine mehendi with a sangeet or follow it with a cocktail night. These evenings bring stage lights, dance performances and dark backgrounds. Use darker templates with subtle gold or bokeh textures, and choose photos with clear faces; dramatic stage lighting can leave faces in shadow. A full-width dance photo across a 12x36 spread captures the energy well, provided no face sits on the fold.

Detail shots that tell the story

Haldi and mehendi pages come alive with details: the turmeric bowl, marigold strings, bangles, the mehendi cone, sweets and decorated swings. Group four to six details into a strip or grid on one side of a spread to balance a large candid photo on the other side.

Consistency with the main album

Even though these pages are more playful, keep the same font family, margin width and gutter spacing as the ceremony chapters. Viewers should sense a change in mood without feeling they have opened a different book. A small chapter title using the album's script font is enough to mark the transition.

Common mistakes

  • Too many similar candids: choose the best expression from each burst.
  • Yellow overload: avoid yellow backgrounds behind haldi photos.
  • Missing the mehendi design: always include one sharp close-up of the full pattern.

Plan the order of photos within each chapter to follow the event as it happened: setup and details first, then the bride or groom, then family joining in, then the celebration. A natural sequence makes even playful pages easy to follow, and it helps relatives relive the day in order.

Frequently asked questions

How many pages should haldi and mehendi get in an album?

Two to four spreads each is usually enough so the main ceremony still has space.

What background suits haldi photos?

Light cream, pale yellow or white backgrounds let the bright haldi colours stand out without overwhelming the page.

How do I fix yellow skin in haldi photos?

Set white balance manually from a neutral white object, then adjust skin with a masked Hue/Saturation layer if needed.

Which templates suit mehendi pages?

Floral and jewel-toned templates suit mehendi; light cream templates suit haldi.

Can I combine haldi and mehendi on one spread?

For smaller albums, yes. Use a clear visual divide and a short title for each event.

Should haldi pages come before mehendi in the album?

Follow the order in which the events happened at that wedding. In many families mehendi comes first, in others haldi does, so confirm with the couple.
