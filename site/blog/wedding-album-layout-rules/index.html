Wedding Album Layout Rules Professionals Follow

Good album layouts look effortless, but they follow a handful of rules that experienced designers apply almost automatically. Learning these rules makes any template look better and helps you adapt layouts to the photos you actually have.

By Guru Moorthi · Updated 2026-10-22 · 3 min read

1. One focal point per spread

Every spread should have one image that the eye lands on first, usually the largest and strongest photo. Supporting images are smaller and placed so they lead the eye back to the hero. If two photos are the same size and fighting for attention, make one bigger.

2. Respect the fold

Never put a face, a name or a key detail across the centre fold. Treat the left and right pages as two halves that work together, and keep a clear zone around the fold.

3. Use a grid and align everything

Frames should share edges and spacing. Use guides to set margins and gutters, and keep the gap between photos consistent across the album. Misaligned frames a few pixels apart look careless in print.

4. Margins create a premium look

Leave breathing room around the edges and between photos. Tight, edge-to-edge collages can work for energetic events, but most spreads look more expensive with generous margins. Keep important content inside the safe area so trimming does not cut it.

5. Match frame shapes to photo orientation

Put vertical photos in vertical frames and horizontal photos in horizontal frames. Cropping a landscape group shot into a tall frame removes people. If a template's frames do not match your photos, adjust the frames or choose another layout.

6. Tell the story in order

Arrange spreads chronologically by event, and within a spread, place photos so the sequence reads left to right. A thali-tying sequence or a reception entry reads naturally when the moments flow across the page.

7. Pair photos that belong together

Photos on the same spread should share lighting, colour and mood. A bright outdoor portrait next to a dark stage photo looks mismatched. If you must combine them, match their colour with clipped adjustment layers.

8. Vary the rhythm

Alternate dense spreads with simpler ones. After two spreads of ritual photos, give the viewer a single full-width portrait. This rhythm keeps the album interesting from start to finish.

9. Limit decoration

Ornaments, borders and textures should support the photos. Hide decorations that sit behind faces or draw attention away from the hero image. Often, using half the ornaments in a template looks more refined.

10. Consistency across the album

Use one font pair, a small colour palette and one template family per event. Consistency is what makes an album feel like one book rather than thirty separate designs.

Applying the rules to templates

Templates from the wedding album design PSD collection already follow many of these rules. Your job is to keep them intact while placing your photos: do not force a photo into a frame of the wrong orientation, do not fill every frame and do not add extra ornaments. Then check each spread against these rules before export, and run the print-ready checklist.
