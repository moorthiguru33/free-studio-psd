Colour Grading Wedding Album Photos

Colour correction fixes problems; colour grading creates a mood. A wedding album with consistent grading feels like one story rather than a stack of photos from different cameras and lighting conditions. This guide shows a practical grading workflow for album designers using Lightroom, Camera Raw and Photoshop.

By Guru Moorthi · Updated 2026-10-23 · 4 min read

Correction first, grading second

Grading works only on photos that are already neutral. Fix white balance, exposure and skin tones first; our colour correction tips cover that step. Once the photos match each other, grading adds the creative layer on top.

Choosing a look for the album

Pick one direction for the whole album and stick to it. Common looks for Indian weddings include:

  • Warm and rich: golden highlights, deep reds, slightly lifted shadows. Suits traditional temple and mandap ceremonies.
  • Clean and bright: neutral whites, soft pastels, gentle contrast. Suits daytime and Christian ceremonies.
  • Moody and cinematic: muted greens, teal shadows, warm skin. Suits receptions and evening portraits.

Look at the album templates you plan to use. A golden Karizma background calls for warm grading, while a white floral template suits a clean look. Our wedding album colour themes guide helps match the two.

Building a grade in Lightroom or Camera Raw

Tone curve

A gentle S-curve adds contrast. Lifting the bottom-left point slightly gives a soft, matte black that many couples like. Keep it subtle; heavy fades make prints look washed out.

HSL and colour mixer

Use the Orange slider carefully because it controls skin. Reduce saturation of distracting greens and blues in the background, and shift greens slightly toward yellow for a warmer feel.

Colour grading wheels

Add a little warmth to highlights and a touch of teal or neutral to shadows. Balance around 0 to 20 for most wedding work. Save the result as a preset so every photo starts from the same point.

Applying the grade consistently

Apply the preset to all selected photos, then work event by event. Ceremony photos under yellow lights need different base corrections than outdoor portraits, but the grade on top stays the same. Use the reference view or compare mode to place a hero image beside each photo you adjust.

Grading inside Photoshop

If photos are already placed in album PSDs, add a Curves or Color Lookup adjustment layer at the top of the photo stack and clip it to the photo, not the whole sheet. Grading the entire sheet would also shift the template background and ornaments, which usually looks wrong.

Using LUTs

Color Lookup adjustment layers can apply a LUT file. Reduce the layer opacity to 40 to 70 percent so the effect is gentle and skin stays natural.

Protecting skin tones

Indian skin tones vary widely, and heavy grading can make them grey, orange or green. Check faces at 100 percent zoom after every change. If skin shifts, add a masked Hue/Saturation layer to pull it back.

Checking before print

A calibrated monitor is essential; read our monitor calibration guide. Order a test print of one graded spread before the full album, and keep the grade a little lighter than you think, because prints are usually darker than screens.

Templates that suit each look

A sample grading workflow

Here is a simple order many album designers follow for a full wedding:

  • Cull and organise: remove duplicates and blurry frames first.
  • Base correction: white balance, exposure and lens correction on all photos.
  • Match cameras: if two photographers shot the wedding, adjust one camera's colours to match the other using a reference photo of the same scene.
  • Apply the grade: sync your album preset across all photos.
  • Event tweaks: adjust strength for night, indoor and outdoor scenes.
  • Skin check: review portraits at 100 percent.
  • Export: sRGB JPGs sized for album placement.

Mistakes that ruin grading

Over-saturated reds

Bridal sarees and kumkum are already intense reds. Pushing saturation makes them clip and lose texture in print. Reduce red saturation slightly instead.

Heavy teal and orange

The popular cinematic look can turn skin orange and green decorations murky. Use it gently, if at all, for traditional ceremonies.

Different grades per photographer

When multiple shooters deliver photos, mismatched grades are obvious on a spread where their images sit side by side. Always grade the whole set together.

Saving and reusing your look

Save successful grades as named presets, for example Warm Temple or Clean Daylight, and note which album templates they suit. Over time you will build a small, reliable set that speeds up every new album.

For warm grades, try golden album PSDs and Karizma album PSDs. For clean looks, the floral album PSDs and 12x36 wedding album sheets offer lighter backgrounds.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between colour correction and colour grading?

Correction makes colours accurate and consistent; grading adds a creative mood on top of corrected photos.

Should I grade the whole album sheet?

No. Clip grading adjustments to the photos only, otherwise the template background and ornaments also change.

How do I keep skin natural while grading?

Adjust the orange channel carefully, check faces at 100 percent, and lower the strength of LUTs to 40 to 70 percent.

Should I use the same preset for every wedding?

Use a consistent starting preset for your style, but adjust strength and white balance for each wedding's lighting.

Can I grade photos after placing them in the album?

Yes, with clipped adjustment layers in Photoshop, but grading in Lightroom or Camera Raw before placing is faster for large sets.
