Choosing a Colour Theme for a Wedding Album

A consistent colour theme is one of the simplest ways to make an album look designed rather than assembled. When backgrounds, ornaments and text share a small palette that matches the photos, every spread feels connected. Here is how to choose and apply a theme.

By Guru Moorthi · Updated 2026-10-24 · 3 min read

Start from the wedding itself

The best palettes come from the event: the bride's main saree or gown, the groom's outfit, the mandap or stage decor and the venue. Pick two or three dominant colours from a few key photos. In Photoshop, the Eyedropper tool can sample colours directly from images; save them as swatches.

Build a simple palette

  • One main colour: used for backgrounds or large areas (for example ivory, maroon or deep green).
  • One accent colour: used for ornaments, borders and headings (often gold).
  • One neutral: for text and calm areas (cream, white, charcoal).

Use the palette on every spread. You can vary the shade slightly between events while keeping the same family of colours.

Classic Indian wedding palettes

  • Maroon, gold and ivory: traditional and royal.
  • Deep green, gold and cream: suits temple settings and South Indian weddings.
  • Kasavu cream and gold: understated Kerala style.
  • Pastel pink, mint and champagne: modern mehendi and day events.
  • Navy, silver and white: evening receptions.

These are common choices, not rules; the photos should decide.

Different events, related colours

Multi-day weddings often have very different outfits: yellow for haldi, green for mehendi, red for the ceremony, a gown or suit for the reception. Instead of forcing one palette everywhere, give each event its own variation while keeping the same neutral and the same accent colour. Gold or ivory accents can tie together very different event colours.

Recolouring templates

Templates rarely match a couple's colours exactly. Add a Hue/Saturation or Color Balance adjustment layer and clip it to the ornament or background (Alt+Ctrl+G). Shift the hue to your palette and lower saturation for a refined look. Gradient Map adjustment layers can turn a texture into any two-colour combination. These edits are non-destructive and easy to reuse across spreads.

Keep skin tones natural

Strong coloured backgrounds can make skin look off by contrast. A bright green background beside warm skin, for example, can make faces look redder. If faces look wrong next to the background, lower the background's saturation or choose a more neutral shade.

Text colour

Use your neutral or accent colour for text. Gold text on a light background can be hard to read; add a subtle shadow or choose a darker gold. Keep text colour consistent throughout the album.

Test the palette

Before designing the full album, make two or three spreads with the palette and view them side by side at a small size. If they feel connected, continue; if not, adjust before you have thirty spreads to change.
