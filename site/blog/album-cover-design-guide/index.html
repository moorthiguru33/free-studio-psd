Album Cover Design Guide: Making a Strong First Impression

The cover is the first thing a client touches and the image that appears in every photo of the album on social media. A strong cover feels simple and confident; a weak one looks like an inside page squeezed onto the front. This guide covers layout, typography and technical preparation for album covers.

By Guru Moorthi · Updated 2026-10-17 · 3 min read

Choose one hero photo

A cover works best with a single strong image: a close couple portrait, an emotional moment or a striking silhouette. Avoid collages of six or eight photos; small images become unreadable at cover size and compete with the names. If the client insists on multiple photos, use one large image and at most two small supporting ones on the back cover.

Leave space for the names

Plan where the names and date will sit before placing the photo. Common layouts are names centred over a darker area of the photo, names on a solid panel beside the photo, or names in a small band at the bottom. Keep the text away from faces and well inside the safe area. Our album cover dimensions guide explains closed sizes, bleed and wrap margins.

Typography that survives printing

  • Use at most two fonts: one display or script font for names, one clean font for the date or venue.
  • Avoid very thin scripts on textured, leather-look or canvas covers; the fine strokes disappear.
  • Make names large enough to read from arm's length, and check the spelling with the client in writing.
  • Gold or silver foil effects look best with simple, bold letterforms.

See choosing fonts for wedding albums for pairings that work well.

Match the cover to the inside

The cover should feel like the same book as the spreads inside. Pick a colour from the opening spread for the name panel, or reuse an ornament from the template family you used inside. A royal gold album with a minimal black-and-white cover can work, but it should be a deliberate choice, not an accident.

Front-only, wrap-around and acrylic covers

Labs offer different cover types, and each needs a different file. A printed front panel needs only the front size plus bleed. A wrap-around cover needs back, spine and front in one file with the spine width for that page count. Acrylic or glass covers often show the photo through a clear panel, so the edges of the photo may not be visible. Always download the lab's current template and follow its guides.

Spine and back cover

Keep the spine simple: names or initials and perhaps the year, centred and readable when the album stands on a shelf. The back cover can carry a quieter photo, a small studio logo if the client agrees, or simply a matching background.

Technical checklist

  • Correct closed size for the album (12x18 for a 12x36 album).
  • Bleed included and background extended into it.
  • Text and faces inside the safe area.
  • sRGB JPG or the format the lab requests, at 300 DPI if the lab allows.
  • Fonts rasterised or embedded in the print copy.

Using a cover template

Start with a layout from the album cover PSD collection, clip your hero photo into the main frame, edit the names, then hide any ornaments that clutter the design. Simple covers usually age better than heavily decorated ones.
